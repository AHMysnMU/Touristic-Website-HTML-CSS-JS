Eiffel Tower

Short history

Built for the 1889 Exposition Universelle, the Eiffel Tower was conceived as a temporary gateway that would demonstrate France’s industrial prowess. Designed by Gustave Eiffel’s engineering company, the iron lattice structure challenged expectations about scale and aesthetics. Early critics questioned its appearance, but the tower quickly became an icon of modern engineering and urban identity. Its design relies on open trusses and carefully calculated curves that help distribute wind loads. The modular iron components were fabricated with precision and assembled on-site, showcasing the efficiencies of industrial construction. Over the decades, the Eiffel Tower proved useful beyond tourism: it supported scientific experiments, meteorological observations, and radio communications—functions that helped secure its long-term preservation. Today, the tower represents the intersection of art, engineering, and public imagination. Whether viewed from afar as a skyline anchor or experienced up close through its platforms and stairways, it continues to symbolize innovation and the evolving character of Paris.
