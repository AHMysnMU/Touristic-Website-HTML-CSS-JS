Taj Mahal

Short history

Commissioned by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal, the Taj Mahal is a masterpiece of symmetry and craftsmanship. Constructed primarily of white marble and completed around 1653, the mausoleum anchors a larger garden complex that expresses ideals of balance, order, and paradise imagery common in Mughal design. The building’s central dome, flanking minarets, and delicately carved surfaces create a sense of harmony from multiple viewpoints. Decorative elements include inlaid stonework, calligraphic inscriptions, and geometric patterns that shift subtly with daylight. The surrounding charbagh garden layout organizes paths, water channels, and plantings to frame the monument while guiding visitors toward the main structure. Beyond its architectural elegance, the Taj Mahal represents a fusion of Persian, Islamic, and Indian influences. It remains an enduring symbol of devotion and artistry, attracting visitors who come to study its materials, proportions, and the way its façade transforms across seasons and time of day.

Key facts
