Great Pyramid of Giza

Short history

Rising from the edge of the Sahara, the Great Pyramid of Giza is the oldest and largest of the pyramids on the Giza Plateau. Built during Egypt’s Fourth Dynasty for Pharaoh Khufu, it was designed as a monumental tomb and as a statement of royal power. For centuries it stood as the tallest human-made structure on Earth, inspiring travelers, scholars, and builders across cultures. Although its original smooth casing stones were largely removed over time, the pyramid’s core masonry still conveys remarkable precision. Its internal passages and chambers reflect a sophisticated understanding of geometry, stoneworking, and logistics. Archaeological evidence suggests a large, organized workforce of skilled laborers rather than enslaved builders, supported by nearby settlements and supply networks. Today, the Great Pyramid remains a symbol of ancient engineering and enduring mystery. It invites visitors to consider how a civilization, using primarily simple tools and careful planning, achieved an architectural feat that continues to shape global imagination.

Key facts

Location
