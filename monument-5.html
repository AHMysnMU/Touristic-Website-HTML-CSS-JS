Machu Picchu

Short history

Perched high in the Andes, Machu Picchu is a remarkable Inca site that blends architecture with dramatic natural topography. Believed to have been built in the mid-15th century, it features finely cut stone structures, ceremonial spaces, and agricultural terraces that stabilized steep slopes. The site’s planning reflects Inca expertise in water management, with channels and fountains designed to guide rainfall and spring water through the settlement. Machu Picchu’s exact original function remains debated; interpretations range from a royal estate to a religious retreat or administrative center. What is clear is the technical skill evident in its stonework. Many walls use tightly fitted blocks without mortar, a technique that increased stability and helped structures endure seismic activity. The site was abandoned during the period of Spanish conquest and remained largely unknown to the wider world until the early 20th century. Today, Machu Picchu is valued for its cultural significance and for how its terraces and pathways reveal a civilization’s ability to engineer sustainably within a challenging mountain environment.
